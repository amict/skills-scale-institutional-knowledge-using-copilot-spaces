# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## About OctoAcme project management

OctoAcme follows a structured, customer-first approach to project delivery built around iterative execution, clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and continuous learning. The organization emphasizes customer value and usability, small testable increments, and a consistent project rhythm that helps teams move from idea to delivery without losing visibility or quality. Each initiative is expected to have a named Project Manager and Product Lead, with clear responsibilities for planning, execution, risk management, and communication.

The project management process spans five core phases: Initiation, Planning, Execution, Release, and Retrospective. In practice, this means defining the problem and stakeholders early, aligning scope and milestones before work begins, tracking progress with strong team communication, validating quality before release, and using retrospectives to capture lessons learned and improve the next cycle. The overall philosophy is to combine clarity, accountability, and psychological safety so the team can deliver value consistently and adapt quickly when priorities or risks change.

## Process summary

OctoAcme relies on a repeatable project rhythm to keep cross-functional teams aligned. Work begins with initiation and planning, where the goals, stakeholders, scope, and success metrics are made explicit. During execution, teams use standups, sprint or milestone demos, project boards, and PR-based collaboration to track progress and manage dependencies. Risk and communication are managed through a risk register, weekly updates, and clearly defined escalation paths so issues are surfaced early and addressed with the right stakeholders.

Quality is treated as a delivery requirement rather than a final checkpoint. The team expects clear acceptance criteria, automated validation in CI, security checks, and manual QA where needed. Before release, all prerequisites must be met, including passing tests, documenting rollback plans, and verifying the release in staging and production. After deployment, the team closes the loop with retrospectives and improvements so lessons are incorporated into future project cycles.
